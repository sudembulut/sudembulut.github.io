About

Reading the signal

I study Mathematical Engineering at Istanbul Technical University, graduating in 2027. The thing that keeps pulling me back is estimation under uncertainty — how you recover something true from measurements that are incomplete, noisy, or both. It shows up in the coursework, and it turns out to be most of the job in practice too.

The jobs so far have all sat where data meets a decision. I spent a year as a business intelligence engineering intern at Amazon, on the TR Retail Tech team, working on efficiency, automation and applied AI. Before that, customer experience analytics at Getir — reporting metrics to the executive team and maintaining the dashboards behind them — and market research at StartGate, an accelerator for digital games across the EU and MENAT region.

My research sits on the same question in physical form: whether you can estimate how crowded an outdoor space is using cheap radios instead of cameras. It's funded under TÜBİTAK's 2209-A programme, and it's the work I'm most invested in right now — partly for the signal processing, partly because it can be done without watching anyone.

Teaching, mostly

The through-line in everything outside work is handing things over to other people. I spent three years mentoring a robotics team through the STEAM curriculum, competing at the 2025 World Championship in the United States. Before university I guided twenty-six high-school students from across Anatolia through Harvard's CS50x, writing weekly games and quizzes to keep an online classroom alive through the pandemic.

I also rebuilt the ITU chapter of Toplum Gönüllüleri Vakfı after a five-year dormancy, growing it into a volunteer network of more than a hundred people. Organising is a different discipline from engineering, and I'd argue the harder one.
